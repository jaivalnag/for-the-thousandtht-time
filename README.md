Real Estate Aquisitions DCF Model
Project Overview
The DCF Model is a dynamic financial modeling tool engineered to evaluate the feasibility of multifamily real estate acquisitions. Moving beyond static spreadsheets, this application provides a responsive interface for stress-testing investment assumptions in real-time, allowing analysts to quantify the impact of market volatility on investor returns.

This tool was developed as a "Proof of Work" asset to demonstrate the intersection of quantitative finance and real estate underwriting.

📊 Core Financial Metrics
The engine provides instant feedback on the "Big Five" KPIs used by institutional investment committees:

Internal Rate of Return (IRR): Calculated using an iterative approximation to determine the annualized rate of earnings.

Net Present Value (NPV): Measures the value creation above the target 8.5% discount rate (hurdle rate).

Equity Multiple (EM): Quantifies total cash inflows relative to the initial equity outlay.

Average Cash-on-Cash Return: Measures the annual levered liquidity generated by the asset.

Stabilized Development Yield: Calculates the NOI relative to total project cost to determine the "yield-on-cost" spread.

🛠️ Key Features
1. Leveraged Cash Flow Waterfall
A full Year 1–5 waterfall that calculates:

PGI to EGI: Transitioning from Potential Gross Income to Effective Gross Income after accounting for Vacancy and Credit Loss.

NOI Generation: Subtracting Operating Expenses (OpEx) to find the Net Operating Income.

Leveraged CFBT: Accounting for annual debt service on a 65% LTC loan to arrive at Cash Flow Before Tax.

2. 3x3 Sensitivity Analysis Matrix
A sophisticated risk-management module that stress-tests the project's IRR against two primary variables:

Exit Cap Rate (X-Axis): Increments of +/- 50 basis points from the base case.

Vacancy Rate (Y-Axis): Increments of +/- 100 basis points from the base case.

3. Reversion (Exit) Logic
The tool automatically calculates the Terminal Value based on the Year 6 projected NOI and the input Exit Cap Rate, then subtracts selling costs and the remaining loan balance to find the Net Sale Proceeds.

💡 Underwriting Thesis (Base Case)
The pre-loaded defaults represent a theoretical $20,000,000 Value-Add Acquisition:

Strategy: Forced appreciation through $8M in capital improvements (Hard & Soft Costs).

Rent Upside: Capturing market gaps through unit interior renovations and repositioning.

Conservative Modeling: Using an OpEx growth rate that accounts for inflationary pressures, ensuring the model remains grounded in reality.
